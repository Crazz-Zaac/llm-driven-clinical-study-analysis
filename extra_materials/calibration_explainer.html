How to read the calibration plot (Figure 15)

RAGAS answer correctness vs. manually confirmed correct answers. Work through the four steps: the real plot, one bin up close, what happens with different papers, and a short reading checklist.

Step 1 · The real figure

Each point is one bin of answers

The reported cells were sorted into 10 bins by their RAGAS score (0–0.1, 0.1–0.2, …). A point sits at the average score of its bin (x) and the share you judged correct (y). The bar is the 95 % Wilson interval. Hover over a point: dashed guides run to both axes and show its exact score and percentage. Each shaded band is one bin, and the gridlines are every 10 %. Click a point to open it in Step 2.

Step 3 · What a different set of papers would show

Why small bins jump around

Pretend the observed share of each bin is the true rate (green tick). Then draw new cells for every bin, with the same number of cells, as if you had evaluated other papers. Small bins (few cells) jump a lot, large bins barely move. About 95 % of the drawn bars should contain the green tick; a bar that misses it turns red.

Step 4 · Reading checklist

Five questions to ask of this plot

1. Does the line go up?

Yes, in both panels. A higher RAGAS score means a higher chance that the answer is correct, so the score ranks answers sensibly. The AUC (0.88 / 0.84 for V1, 0.76 / 0.75 for V2) summarizes how well. Ignore single wiggles; look at the overall trend.

2. Is a wiggle real? (Llama V1: 36 % at 0.3–0.4, then 29 % at 0.4–0.5)

Compare the bars: 23–50 % and 18–42 % overlap almost completely. The dip is noise from small bins, not a finding. Rule of thumb: if two neighbouring bars overlap a lot, don't interpret the difference between their dots.

3. Is RAGAS too harsh or too lenient at some score?

Compare with the dotted diagonal. Where the whole bar lies above it, answers are more often correct than their score suggests (too harsh). V1 Llama at 0.7–0.8: 94 % correct, bar 79–98 %, clearly above 0.73. Where the bar crosses the diagonal, you can't tell.

4. What does the threshold do?

Everything left of the dashed line at 0.75 is labelled "flagged". In V1, the bins between 0.5 and 0.75 are 52–94 % correct, and all of them are rejected. That's where most judge false negatives come from: the score ranks well, the cut-off is too high.

5. Do the two judges behave the same?

No. Switch Step 1 to V2: the ministral line is flatter (worse separation) and hardly any cells score above 0.8, so a 0.75 threshold rejects almost everything. A threshold therefore can't be transferred between judges.

Bonus: why do the Llama and MedLlama points sit at different x positions?

The bins are identical. Each point is drawn at the average score of the cells in its bin, which differs between generators (e.g. 0.73 vs 0.75 in the 0.7–0.8 bin). In the thesis figure, the points are also shifted slightly sideways (±0.008) so the bars don't overlap. The shaded bands in Step 1 show that both points of a bin lie in the same band.
